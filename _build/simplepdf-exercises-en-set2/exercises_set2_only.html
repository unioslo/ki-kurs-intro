Set 2

Exercises for exploring the possibilities in your own everyday work.

Map your own work tasks

Step 1

Write down 5-10 work tasks that you do over the course of a working week. (E.g. responding to emails, creating presentations, writing or updating website text, preparing reports or case documents, planning teaching, seminars or meetings, supervising students, data processing / analysis, case handling)

Step 2

Write a few keywords about what the task involves, how often you do it, and how much time you spend on it.

Step 3

Mark which tasks are:

  • routine
  • text-heavy
  • difficult to get an overview of
  • require a lot of ideation

Step 4

Assess the risks of using AI for these work tasks.

  1. Do you process personal data?
  2. Do you process data that must be protected, such as research secrets or details about critical UiO infrastructure?
  3. Do you process data that is protected by copyright?
  4. If the AI makes a mistake, would it have unfortunate or serious consequences?

Step 5

Choose 2-3 tasks where AI has the potential to make things more efficient or improve them, and explore the following:

  1. How can AI be used? Feel free to test it together with a colleague.
  2. What must you do yourself?
  3. Assess what kind of risks can arise from using AI for this task.
  4. If the task involves risk: Think through and write down the necessary measures to reduce and control the risk.

Step 6

After exploring your work tasks above, we encourage you to try using AI for the tasks where you see the greatest potential.

Step 7

Identify 2-3 tasks where AI should not be used, and reflect on why.
