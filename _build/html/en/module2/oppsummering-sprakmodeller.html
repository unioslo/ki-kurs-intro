Summary of chapter 2

In this chapter you have learned how large language models produce text and what weaknesses they have when they do this. Before you move on to chapter 3, you can test what you have learned.

Test yourself

Discuss on your own or with a colleague

  1. What are large language models and how do they work?
  2. Name some of the most important weaknesses of large language models and what you can do to address them.

Recap

  • Large language models are mathematical models that build up their answer one word at a time, conditioned on the context you give them. In other words, large language models generate text based on statistical patterns, rather than certain factual knowledge.
  • Large language models have no relationship to truth and therefore have no reliable knowledge of what is true. This makes the models vulnerable to deliberate manipulation of training data. Actors can publish misleading information so that the models will be trained on it. As a result, the models can give answers that are manipulated and do not match reality.
  • Language models make up things that are not true because they do not have certain factual knowledge.
  • Long conversations can “confuse” the model, so that it gives poorer answers. That is why it is important to start a new conversation when you change topic.
  • Language models have built-in biases. These can include, among others, bias resulting from a lack of training data, bias that mirrors biases in society, or confirmation bias.
