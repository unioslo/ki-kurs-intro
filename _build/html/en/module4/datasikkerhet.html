Data security

Before you use an AI service to process text, documents or other information, there are two things you need to consider:

  1. Is the AI service approved by UiO?
  2. What data class does the information you want to use belong to?

If the AI service is not approved by UiO, you may only process green data. This applies no matter how you use the service, for example when you

  • paste text into a chat
  • upload a document
  • use an AI assistant
  • ask the AI to summarise, translate or process content.

Most AI services out there send your data on to external suppliers. Your information may then be stored or processed outside UiO’s control. You avoid this, however, if you choose one of UiO’s approved AI services .

The most important thing you can do to safeguard data security is therefore to choose an approved AI service and check which data class the service can handle. You will learn more about UiO’s AI services in chapter 5.

UiO’s data classification

UiO’s AI services are approved for use with different types of data. This is clearly indicated in each service. Which service you can use depends on the level of protection your data requires.

Below you will find an overview of the data classes with examples of the types of information that belong to each class. You will find more detailed information on the page How to classify data and information .

Use the overview together with UiO’s web page on data classification to work out which data class your data belongs to, and then choose an AI service that is approved for that data class.

🟢 Green: Open information

“Open” is used when it does not cause any harm to public interests, the university, an individual or a partner if the information becomes known to unauthorised persons or is changed unintentionally.

Information that can be shared with everyone.

For example:

  • many public websites
  • published research data
  • public documents
  • open access articles

🟡 Yellow: Restricted information

“Restricted” is used when it could cause some harm to public interests, the university, an individual or a partner if the information becomes known to unauthorised persons or is changed unintentionally.

For example:

  • personal data
  • student assignments
  • documents on Canvas or similar platforms
  • lecture notes
  • research articles behind a paywall

🔴 Red: Confidential information

“Confidential” is used when it could cause harm to public interests, the university, an individual or a partner if the information becomes known to unauthorised persons or is changed unintentionally.

Information that may be accessible, but only to those with approved access.

For example:

  • sensitive personal data
  • health information
  • upcoming exam questions

⚫ Black: Strictly confidential information

“Strictly confidential” is used when it could cause significant harm to public interests, the university, an individual or a partner if the information becomes known to unauthorised persons or is changed unintentionally.

Information with very limited access.

For example:

  • large amounts of red data
  • highly confidential research data

The data class tells you which AI services you can use. If the data contains personal data, the privacy rules apply in addition.
