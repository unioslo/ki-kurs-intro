Summary of chapter 4

In this chapter you have learned about important and challenging environmental and ethical aspects of using artificial intelligence. You have also refreshed your knowledge of the rules and guidelines you should follow when using the technology. With increased knowledge of this, you can use AI in a more responsible way.

Test yourself

Discuss on your own or with a colleague.

  • What can you do to reduce the environmental impact of AI services in your day-to-day work?
  • What types of data do you handle in your work? Try to classify them according to the data classes.
  • Think about how you use AI. Have you been unsure whether what you upload to AI complies with UiO’s rules and policies?
  • Reflect on how you use AI. Does AI strengthen or weaken you professionally?

Recap

  • AI is a resource-intensive technology. The enormous demand for AI leads to high consumption of electricity, water and land. You can reduce the burden by being aware of when you use AI, choosing a smaller language model for simple tasks and starting a new conversation when the conversation starts to get long.
  • There are social costs associated with AI. Like all large-scale industry, AI has social costs, both during production and in use.
  • Use AI services approved by UiO. Most AI services are cloud services, and data you share can fall into the wrong hands. By using UiO’s approved AI services, you can be confident this will not happen.
  • Personal data requires more than an approved service. The privacy rules apply no matter which tool you use. You must have a legal basis for processing, and you cannot use the data for purposes other than those for which it was collected.
  • Copyright also applies when you use AI. Many AI services store the text they receive and may use it to train new models. You are protected against this if you use UiO’s approved AI services. You must nonetheless always check whether the material you upload permits uploading to AI.
  • External content can contain hidden instructions. Websites and documents you let a language model read may try to steer it. This is called prompt injection. Use sources you trust, and always check the answer before you use it.
  • You are responsible for how you use AI tools, and you must familiarise yourself with the regulations. UiO’s IT regulations also apply to AI, and in addition UiO has its own AI guidelines that must be followed.

Next chapter
